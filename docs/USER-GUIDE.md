# 🍋 Lemon Piano — how to play

*The instruction sheet. Written to be printed and left next to the piano, so it
explains the box in front of you and assumes nothing about the code inside it.*

Applies to the **V5.5** board (the one in the printed enclosure) with the
2026-09-09 firmware. Spanish translation: [GUIA-DE-USO.es.md](GUIA-DE-USO.es.md).

---

## 1. Set it up

1. Push one nail into each of the seven lemons and clip a wire to each nail,
   left to right — that is key **1** to key **7**.
2. Plug the eighth wire — the loose one — into the **clip**, and **hold the clip
   in one hand**. This is not optional: you are half the circuit. Nothing works
   until someone is holding it.
3. Power it from a **USB wall charger**, not from a laptop. A computer's ground
   noise walks straight past the filter and the piano starts playing by itself.
4. Touch a lemon with your other hand. It sings.

> **If nothing happens, or if it plays without being touched**, jump to
> §6 *Tuning*. Fruit changes as it dries out; the piano expects to be re-tuned.

## 2. Switching on

The bar of ten lights fills up on its own for a couple of seconds and you hear
a coin for each lemon. That is **calibration**: it is measuring what "not being
touched" looks like for each lemon.

**Keep your hands off the fruit while the lights are running.** If you are
holding a lemon during those two seconds, it learns that as normal and that key
goes deaf.

A little fanfare, and then it plays a short tune — that is the piano telling you
**which mode it woke up in**. It always wakes up in **Level 1**.

## 3. The game

Each level hides a **secret ten-note melody**. Your job is to find it by ear.

- Touch lemons freely: nothing bad happens and the bar stays empty.
- The moment you hit the **first note of the secret melody**, light 1 comes on.
  You are in.
- Every further correct note lights the next light. **1 → 10.**
- A wrong note **blanks the whole bar** and you start the melody again. You will
  hear the note you played first, then the "wrong" rattle — so you always know
  which lemon was the mistake.
- Light all ten and the level's own theme plays, with the lights counting up to
  it, then the flagpole fanfare — and the **next level starts by itself**.
- Clear level 4 and the finale plays on a loop until you stop it (see §5).

**Playing the same lemon twice in a row is not a mistake — it just does not
count.** The note sounds exactly as it should; the game simply is not listening
for it, because it is already waiting for a *different* lemon.

You can see that it did not count: **one light runs backwards across the bar**,
from the right end to the left, and then your score comes back exactly as it
was. Backwards, because it took you nowhere. Nothing is lost, nothing is
deducted, and there is no telling-off noise — that rattle is gone (2026-09-13).
Compare it with a wrong note, which **blanks the bar and leaves it blank**: the
two can never be confused.

## 4. Free play — the piano with no game

Free play is not a level and you cannot reach it by winning. **Hold the + button
for three seconds** and the piano swaps the game for the instrument. Hold it
again and the game comes back, at the level you left.

In free play the seven lemons are simply the notes you were taught at school:

| Lemon | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Note | **do** | **re** | **mi** | **fa** | **sol** | **la** | **si** |
| | C5 | D5 | E5 | F5 | G5 | A5 | B5 |

- No secret melody, no wrong notes, nothing to win.
- **Play the same lemon as many times as you like** — the game's one-touch rule
  is off here, because that rule is the opposite of what an instrument needs.
- **Hold a lemon and the note holds with it — one note, not a stutter.** Let go
  properly and touch it again for a second note. Playing fast still works; it
  only ignores the flicker of a finger that never left.
- The lights become a **pitch meter**: low notes light a little of the bar, high
  notes light all of it.
- **Two lemons at once sound as two notes.** Hold a second lemon along with the
  first and you get the interval, with the bar showing two separate lights, one
  for each note. Let either one go and the other keeps sounding. It takes two
  deliberate touches — a finger resting near a neighbouring lemon will not do
  it, on purpose.
- When you are not playing, the bar is **dark**. Every light you see was put
  there by your own finger.
- Going into free play is announced by the bar itself: two lights run in from
  the ends, meet, open out until the whole bar is lit, and then go out — and
  then the piano plays you the scale.

## 5. Choosing a mode

Everything below uses only the two buttons: **+** and **−**. The rule that makes
them easy to remember: **a tap is the sensitivity, a hold is a mode.**

### The instrument switch — hold **+**

**Hold the + button for three seconds.** From one second the bar fills and a
chirp climbs, exactly like the wheel below, so you can always tell it is
charging. At three seconds the piano swaps:

- playing a level → **free play**, announced by a run up do-re-mi-fa-sol-la-si
- in free play → **back to the level you left**, announced by its own theme

Let go early and nothing happens. It is a switch, so the same gesture takes you
both ways; you never have to go looking for a menu to get your instrument back.

### The level wheel — hold **−**

**Hold the − button down and keep holding.** After one second the piano starts
charging: the bar fills up and you hear a chirp that climbs. At **three seconds**
the lights sweep once across and back, a rising three-note cue plays, and you
are in.

Let go before three seconds and nothing happens — you get a low double bump
meaning "cancelled". *(The press itself turns the sensitivity down one step, as
any − press would; the moment the hold becomes a mode change that step is put
straight back.)*

### Turn it

**Tap +** for the next level, **tap −** for the previous one. It is a wheel: past
level 4 you come back to level 1. Free play is **not** on the wheel — it is the
+ hold above, so turning the wheel can never take your instrument away.

Each stop tells you where you are, twice over:

| Mode | Lights | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 | **1** light, blinking | the Overworld theme |
| Level 2 | **2** lights, blinking | the Underworld theme |
| Level 3 | **3** lights, blinking | the Starman theme |
| Level 4 | **4** lights, blinking | the Castle theme |

Blinking lights mean *a question*; steady lights mean *a score*.

Tap again while a tune is playing and it stops immediately and moves on — you
never have to wait for the music.

### Choose it

**Press both buttons at once.** Order and timing do not matter: press one, then
the other whenever you like. A short confirmation plays and the mode starts.

Choosing a **level** starts it from scratch, with an empty bar. Choosing **free
play** hands you the instrument.

### Leave without choosing

Three ways, all of which change nothing:

- **Hold either button for three seconds** — the opening cue plays backwards.
- **Walk away.** After twenty seconds with no button, it closes itself.
- **Choose the mode you were already in** — it opens on the mode you are
  playing, so opening and immediately accepting does nothing at all.

The game is exactly where you left it: same level, same lights, same progress.

## 6. Tuning — when the fruit stops behaving

The piano reads a touch as a **drop of three or four counts** out of 1024. That
is a tiny signal, and it changes with the fruit, the room and your hands.

| Symptom | Do this |
|---|---|
| Lemons do not respond | Tap **+** a few times (more sensitive) |
| It plays notes on its own | Tap **−** a few times (less sensitive) |
| Neither helps | **Smart adjust**, below |
| Nothing at all works | Are you holding the clip? Is it plugged into a wall charger? |

Each tap moves it one step and the bar shows you where the setting sits; the
chirp's pitch rises as it gets more sensitive, so you can tune it without
looking. Hold a button to run through the steps quickly.

**Smart adjust** — the good one. **Touch a lemon and keep touching it**, then
hold **both buttons for one second**. The bar fills as it listens, and it works
out the best setting from your actual finger on your actual fruit.

- A rising triad (**1-up**) = it learned something. You are done.
- A falling rattle = it could not tell your touch from the noise, and **nothing
  changed**. Check the clip is in your hand and try again.

> **The three long presses, and how to keep them apart.** *Both* buttons for one
> second = tune. *Just* **+** for three seconds = the instrument switch. *Just*
> **−** for three seconds = the level wheel. They cannot collide: if you reach
> for the second button mid-charge, the charge cancels and hands over to tuning.

To start completely fresh, **unplug it and plug it back in**. That re-runs
calibration and returns to level 1.

## 7. Every gesture, on one line each

| Gesture | What it does |
|---|---|
| Tap **+** | More sensitive, one step — **one more light** |
| Tap **−** | Less sensitive, one step — **one light out** |
| Hold **+** for **3 s** | **Free play ⇄ the level** |
| Hold **−** for **3 s** | **Opens the level wheel** |
| In the wheel: tap **+** / **−** | Next / previous level (it wraps) |
| In the wheel: **both buttons** | Choose this mode |
| In the wheel: hold either **3 s** | Leave, change nothing |
| Hold **both** for **1 s**, touching a lemon | Smart adjust |
| Hold **both** for **1 s** during the finale | Back to level 1 |
| Unplug and plug back in | Full reset, re-calibrates |

## 8. Sounds, and what they mean

Every non-musical sound is a Super Mario Bros effect, pitched **above** the
lemons so it can never be mistaken for a note.

| Sound | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Coin, one per lemon | Calibrating — hands off |
| Mushroom power-up | Calibration finished, go ahead |
| Rising chirps, climbing | A button is charging: **+** the instrument switch, **−** the level wheel |
| Rising three notes | The wheel is open |
| The same three, falling | The wheel closed, nothing changed |
| Fifth resolving to the octave | You chose a mode |
| Low double bump | End of the range, or a cancelled charge |
| Short death rattle | Wrong note |
| 1-up | Smart adjust learned something |
| Full death tune | Smart adjust could not; nothing changed |
| Flagpole fanfare | Level cleared |
